# A commun créneau becomes two séances only where a méthodo is written per niveau

Six of the timetable's commun créneaux produce two séances, one CM1 and one CM2: the
four « Mathématiques » cells and the two RETZ cells (grammaire le lundi, conjugaison le
mardi). Every other commun créneau produces one séance at `level = commun`, which links
the items de programme of both niveaux. That gives 1740 séances for 1532 couples (jour
de classe, créneau).

The rule is *the méthodo, not the programme*. Almost every programme officiel is written
per niveau, so "split wherever the content is per niveau" would have split nearly the
whole timetable — and it would have been wrong: the teacher teaches sciences, anglais and
EPS to the two niveaux together, from one programme read at two levels. What she cannot
teach together is `maths-cm1` against `maths-cm2`, or `retz-cm1` against `retz-cm2`:
those are two different books, on two different notions, in the same 45 minutes.

Splitting is a property of the créneau and is fixed for the whole year, not decided week
by week from whether a séquence happens to reach it. The four maths créneaux of the last
semaine carry no séquence — §4.2 leaves that semaine as marge — and they are still two
séances, because a week grid that changed shape in the last week of June would be a
worse thing to read than one that does not. The price is eight séances de révisions that
say the same thing at both niveaux.
